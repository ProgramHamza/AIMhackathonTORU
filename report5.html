The whole system against baselines (report 5)

Clinical documentation app, blocks 5–8. All numbers come from the evaluation runs in backend/eval/results. Hover a bar to see its value.

The metrics in one sentence each

Answer score
The 15 challenge questions, graded against the expected answers: correct = 1, partial = 0.5 (e.g. the drug without its dose), missing or wrong = 0.
Wrong answers
The screen says something that contradicts the expected answer. Worse than missing: a clinician could act on it.
Needed topics addressed
On the 27 blind prompts: share of the topics the request needs that the screen answers or marks 'not documented'.
Irrelevant topics shown
On the blind prompts: share of clearly irrelevant topics shown anyway. Lower is better.
Contradiction score
Real contradictions found (out of 7 known) minus false alarms, as the challenge counts it.
Traceability
Share of the screen's facts whose quote is found word for word in the document it cites. Checked in code.
Unsupported numbers
Facts containing a number that is not in the cited document: a likely invented or altered value. Checked in code.
Time
From the request to the finished screen, live calls, no cache.
Data sent to the cloud
Characters per request that left the device (after de-identification).

5. Before and after the fix

The first run left 5 answers partial although the search had found every needed passage: the summary line of an item quoted at most 2 of them, older documents were hidden even when they held the detail (the pathology report's receptor percentages, the only HbA1c value), and two passages saying the same thing could fill both slots. The fix: a "current" item (drug lists) quotes every current drug; other items quote verified facts first, from any date, with the date shown; repeated statements are skipped. The judge also now sees up to 700 characters per screen line instead of 260, for every system alike.

What the comparison shows

  • Our system answers all 15 questions correctly, and never invents. No partial, missing or wrong answers; its screens contain no number that is absent from the cited document, and every quote is found word for word.
  • The one-call LLM baseline misses most of what a task needs (needed topics addressed about a fifth of the time on unseen prompts), paraphrases about 1 quote in 8, and finds two contradictions plus one false alarm, although it sees the whole record.
  • The rules baseline is instant and always traceable, but it misses half of the needed topics, cannot say "not documented" reliably and finds no contradictions.
  • Our system with and without the LLM score the same here. The decision graph, the remarker and the verified facts do the work the judge measures; the LLM orders the list and writes the summary panel. It costs about 1.6 s and 2 calls.
  • The 5 partial answers of the first run are fixed (section 5): the search had found every needed passage, but the summary line showed too few of them. The fix changed only which found passages are quoted; the graph and the search are unchanged.
  • 15 of 15 is on questions the team also used while building. The blind prompts (needed topics 100%) are the fairer test of generalisation; a new patient record would be the real one.
  • Data sent to the cloud: our two parallel calls both carry the selected passages, and the summary call repeats them in its template lines, so a request sends more characters than the whole record. It is de-identified either way; removing the repetition is a cheap saving.

How the comparison was made, and its limits

  • Baseline A gets the whole record (8 documents) and the request in one Gemini Flash-Lite call (the same model our final step uses) and returns facts with document IDs and quotes, 'not documented' items and contradictions. No graph, no retrieval, no checks.
  • Baseline B maps words in the request to topics with if/else rules and shows every sentence containing a topic keyword (generic German clinical words, nothing specific to this patient), up to 4 per topic.
  • Grading is done by a Gemini model: the same family as Baseline A, which may favour its style. Traceability, unsupported numbers, time, calls and data volume are checked in code and do not depend on the judge.
  • The team wrote the blind prompts and their needed topics; the known contradictions come from reading the documents and from the evidence backend.
  • All runs used free API keys; no billing was enabled. Every text, including the judge's input, passed the de-identification gate.

What these numbers do not prove

  • The team wrote the test prompts and the expected answers, not doctors. A clinician should review them.
  • Blind sets are small (12–15 prompts), so one prompt moves a score by about 7 points.
  • The final-step check uses our own proxy (item, status, source document, visible), not the jury's reading of the answer text. The jury may judge wording and completeness differently.
  • Gemini answers vary a little between runs; latency depends on the network and the free-tier quota.
